How StoneIQ works

Understand the asset. Decide. Then execute.

StoneIQ runs one loop. It reads your management statements into a true picture of each building's economics, works out which decisions would move that number, and then runs the buildings to the guidelines you set — the whole operation, from a tenant's message to the booked invoice, which posts straight back into the numbers.

01 — Understand

Your statements in. The real economics out.

Upload the annual package your régie already prepares — balance sheet, income statement, rent roll, general ledger. It's good accounting, and StoneIQ takes it as the starting point: reading it in, checking it against its own printed totals, and recasting it into the investor's view of the same year — what the building returns on its value, and which unit earns it.

✓
Net yield, building value, and debt in one place
Net operating income against what the building is actually worth — plus loan-to-value and debt cover, so you see the return on your equity, not just the rent. Use your own valuation if you have one; otherwise StoneIQ derives one from the building's own figures and local price references, and always tells you which it used.
✓
Capital works and interest taken out of the operating result
A renovation and a mortgage payment aren't running costs. StoneIQ separates them, so one big year of works doesn't make a healthy building look like it's losing money.
✓
Down to the individual flat, fully loaded
Each unit carries its own costs plus its share of the building's overhead — so you can finally see which flat subsidises which.
✓
Figures that have to tie out before they count
Every imported statement is reconciled against its own stated totals and flagged green, amber, or red. Anything that doesn't balance waits for you — it never quietly lands in your reporting.
02 — Decide

What would it take to reach 4%? Here is the answer.

Name a return you want from the building. StoneIQ works backwards and shows what each lever would have to deliver to get there — how much rent at turnover, how much cost out, which renovation — and tells you plainly where the ceiling is, so you stop chasing a number this building can't reach.

✓
Ranked against how you want the asset run
Tell StoneIQ once whether you're growing this asset, holding it steady, or preparing to sell. Levers that fit that intent come first; the ones that cut against it sit below a line, visible but never recommended.
✓
Every lever bounded by what's actually achievable
Rent is capped at the local market for that flat's size and condition, occupancy at full, costs at your own best year. When StoneIQ can't establish a limit, it greys the lever out rather than inventing headroom.
✓
Renovate now, wait, or do nothing — costed over ten years
Each option is valued in today's francs, including the rent increase Swiss law lets you pass on after the work, the tax relief, the borrowing, and the risk of leaving it. You get a recommendation and the reasoning behind it.

03 — Execute

Guidelines only count once they run the building.

This is where most of StoneIQ actually lives. Your mandate becomes the settings the platform operates by — what gets approved without you, when a component is due, how far rent moves at turnover — and from there it runs the full job: tenant requests, contractors, leases, legal notices, billing. Every franc posts to the ledger the analysis reads from, so the target you set is the one the building is actually steered to.

✓
Your mandate sets the thresholds
A preserving owner and a growth owner get different approval limits, different renovation timing, different rent policy at turnover — applied to every job, not remembered case by case.
✓
The full day-to-day, not a thin layer
Tenant requests triaged and routed, contractor jobs and quotes, leases signed and renewed, QR-Bills and recurring rent, condition reports at move-in and move-out, correspondence, ancillary costs. Tenants and contractors each get their own portal.
✓
Swiss law applied as work happens
Reference rate moves, rent adjustments, canton lease templates, legal notices — drafted for the right situation, in the right language, without you looking them up.
✓
Which is what makes it consistent
Rent received, invoice paid, work booked — posted as it happens, for every building you have moved across. A one-off analysis tells you the number once; running the operations to the mandate is what makes the building hit it year after year.

Before you commit the money

Model the work, then decide — not the other way round.

Pick a component that's near end of life and the simulator prices the three honest options side by side: do it now, wait three years, or leave it. Cost, the rent uplift Swiss law lets you pass on, the vacancy while works run, and the borrowing all sit in the same calculation.

Doing nothing is priced too
Deferral isn't free — the model carries the risk and lost rent of leaving a component past its life, so "wait" has a number instead of being the default.

Where the guidelines do the work

The queue already knows what you would have said.

This is the screen your manager lives in, and it's the reason the target holds. Each job arrives pre-sorted against your mandate: cleared automatically, held for you, or already scheduled in the plan. Nobody has to remember what you'd want — and nothing waits on your inbox that didn't need to.

Your régie moves faster, not slower
Routine work stops queuing behind an owner who is travelling. The threshold you set once clears it, and you still see every franc of it afterwards.
Urgent work never waits on an inbox
No heating, no water, anything touching habitability: the job proceeds whatever the threshold says, and you are notified as it happens rather than asked first. A guideline that puts a tenant at risk is the wrong guideline.
The full operation, not a dashboard on top
Requests, jobs, leases, notices, QR-Bills and contractor invoices all run here. Once a building is operated in StoneIQ, the ledger behind its reporting is first-hand rather than an annual import — which is what turns a yearly figure into a live one.
Built for Switzerland

Swiss law isn't just paperwork here — it's inside the numbers.

The rules don't only decide which notice to send. They decide what a building is allowed to earn — so they belong in the model, not in a document generator bolted on the side.

🏦
SIX QR-Bill (ISO 20022)
Every rent demand generates a compliant QR payment slip. Tenants pay directly from the portal.
📜
CO 259a / 269d tenancy law
Live SNB reference rate ingestion — which sets both the notice you owe your tenants and the ceiling on the rent lever in your analysis.
📈
Post-renovation rent uplift
The share of major works Swiss law lets you pass on to rent — priced into every renovate-or-defer decision, not discovered afterwards.
🧾
Régie charts of accounts
Every gérance numbers its accounts differently. StoneIQ reads them in French and German on their own terms and keeps their labels, rather than forcing a rewrite.
🗂️
PAIF depreciation standards
Swiss asset taxonomy and residual values — the basis for knowing which components are near end of life, and what that will cost you and when.
🌐
FR / EN today · German next
Canton-specific lease templates, a tenant portal in French and English, and bilingual legal term matching.
Why StoneIQ

What makes it different.

Property software manages buildings. StoneIQ manages the investment — and treats the operations as the way you act on it.

01
Your intent is an input, not an afterthought
Two owners can hold the same building and want opposite things from it. You tell StoneIQ which you are, and it ranks every recommendation against that — instead of pushing the same "optimise everything" advice at everyone.
02
It starts from the statements you already have
Nothing to migrate to get started. Hand it the annual package from your régie and you get a reconciled picture of the building back in minutes, from documents that were already sitting in a drawer. Moving the day-to-day across comes later, if and when you want it.
03
It won't invent a number to fill a gap
Where the data doesn't support a figure, StoneIQ greys it out and says so. Imported statements have to tie to their own printed totals before they reach your reporting. Being usefully wrong is worse than being visibly incomplete.
04
Returns on your equity, not just the rent
Mortgages, interest, and amortisation are part of the model — so you see the return on the money you actually put in, plus loan-to-value and whether the rent comfortably covers the debt.
05
Analysis and execution in one system
The renovation you modelled becomes the plan, the quote request, and the booked invoice — then flows back into the yield. Nothing is retyped between a spreadsheet, a manager's inbox, and an accounting file.
06
Swiss by construction
Reference-rate rent adjustments, the uplift you may pass on after renovation, cantonal lease templates, QR-Bills, depreciation standards, French and English in the product with German next. Built into the model, not translated onto it afterwards.
