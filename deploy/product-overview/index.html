The owner's path
Your building is a financial asset

You can read the yield on every other position you hold. A building answers the same questions — what did it return, what is it worth, what should I do next — but nobody has been asking them on your behalf.

Five steps, in the order an owner actually takes them: from the envelope the régie sends once a year to a costed decision about the asset. The screens below are schematic — the real ones are further down the page.

Declare what you want the asset to do.

Five questions — horizon, appetite for works, income versus value, what you would do with a windfall, when you would sell. They resolve into a profile that every later recommendation is ranked against.

Read the position

Good accounting, recast as an investor's view.

The same figures your régie reports, rearranged into the ones you would ask of any holding: net yield, what the asset is worth, what the debt costs, and which flat or garage earns its keep once costs land where they belong.

Costs are attributed from the ledger's own per-unit lines, not spread by a rule of thumb — so a unit's number is defensible line by line.

5
Decide, then execute

What would it take to get to 4.2%?

Name a yield you want and each lever says what it would have to deliver — rent to market, costs down, the garage let, the fee renegotiated, the renovation done. Levers stop at what the market, the building, and your own best year actually allow, and the last column is the ceiling: the best this asset can do.

Then the work is costed over ten years — invest, defer, or do nothing, in today's francs — and the chosen plan becomes a capex schedule the manager executes against.

  • Capital works and mortgage interest split out of operating result
  • Target × lever matrix with a max-reachable ceiling
  • Levers bounded by market, occupancy, and your own best year
  • Fails closed — an unbounded lever is greyed out, never guessed

  • Divergent co-owners resolve to the cautious call, not an average

The régie's own annual package is the input. It is read, reconciled against its printed totals, and graded before a single figure reaches reporting.

Why Propfolio
What makes it different

Property software manages buildings. Propfolio manages the investment, and treats the operations as the means of acting on it.

→
Owner intent is a first-class input
Two owners can hold the same building and want opposite things from it. The declared strategy ranks every lever and verdict, instead of one generic "optimise everything" recommendation for all.
→
Statements in, analysis out
Onboarding starts from the régie's existing annual package rather than a migration project — good accounting taken as the input, then recast into the investor's view. Extraction is reconciled against the document's own printed totals and graded before anything reaches reporting.
→
One source of truth
Maintenance events, lease data, invoices, and legal cases all live in the same system and cross-reference each other. No more spreadsheet bridges between tools.
→
Swiss law built in
The legal engine isn't a PDF library — it evaluates actual Swiss law rules against live reference rates and building-specific data. Automatically flags and routes legal exposure.
→
All four parties, one platform
Manager, owner, tenant, and contractor each have a role-scoped interface. Communication flows through the platform, not via email chains and WhatsApp groups.
→
Returns on equity, not just bookkeeping
Mortgages, interest, and amortisation are modelled, so owners see the return on capital actually invested — alongside loan-to-value and debt cover. Not a record of what was paid, but a read on what the asset is doing.
→
Scales from 1 to many buildings
The same backend powers the professional manager view and a simplified private landlord mode (Phase 2). No separate product, no data migration needed.
→
Built for AI augmentation
Triage, classification, and legal analysis already use rule-based AI. The platform is architected to add LLM-based triage and self-service resolution as the next layer.
